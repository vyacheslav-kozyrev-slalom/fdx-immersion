## 2. Client snapshot (Stated unless noted)

- **Company:** Evergreen Living Services, senior living operator with a skilled nursing and healthcare component. Owns all its facilities ("our motto since the beginning").
- **Footprint:** 14 communities, just shy of 2,500 residents, mostly East, West and South; mix of urban, suburban and rural (S1, ~04:10).
- **Occupancy:** about 78%, target 88-90% (S1, ~04:26). Falling, mostly across the board, maybe slightly worse in suburban than rural (S2, ~16:24; she'd need to verify).
- **Staff turnover:** 38% (S1, ~04:34).
- **Workforce mix (her anecdotal estimate):** ~60% skilled nursing, ~20% operations, ~20% non-medical care (S1, ~12:05). **No headcount given.**
- **Contact's role:** Chief Innovation Officer; supports AI; the board wants an answer on how AI will shape the future (S2, ~14:36).
- **Cora's stance:** focus on "fixing our own walls," not leapfrogging competitors; competitors face the same issues (anecdotal).
- **Budget:** budgets are tighter; she has no budget or cost breakdown to share and said the team can work from assumptions (S1, ~17:10). Broader advertising is not in the budget or her purview (CMO's realm); she's "not opposed."

## 3. Problems Cora described

1. Rising operating costs, especially **food and staffing** (S1, ~05:51); also heating fuel and gas/oil (S2, ~15:27).
2. **Falling occupancy**; people staying home or choosing "boutique" options.
3. **Aging workforce and 38% turnover**; nurses leaving for other careers (for example med spas), not mainly competitors; hiring is in-house and a rising cost; community college pathways are a "slow burn."
4. **Tighter budgets.**
5. **Medicare/Medicaid changes** around the turn of the year may reduce residents' ability to pay.
6. **Board pressure for an AI answer.**
7. Secondary: nurse documentation is time-consuming (she's unsure it's the best place to invest); no standardized resident/family feedback.

**Number one (my judgment, not hers):** falling occupancy (78% against a market near 90%). Runner-up: staffing. Cora did not rank; the PRD leads with staffing and does not mention occupancy. 🔎 REVIEW with Cora.

## 4. How the business works (Stated)

**Intake process**
- Most new residents come by **informal resident-to-friend referral** (friends, neighbors, church); no medical or partner referrals; light regional radio and mailers.
- Counselors complete an **intake form** during the conversation (name, address, nearby family who would visit). Families don't fill in anything formal until they decide.
- Prospects usually call ahead; walk-ins are more common in urban/suburban. First tour, optional follow-up tours, follow-up after the first visit.
- Decision cycle typically **3-6 months**; "the ball is in their court" while they tour other places.
- No trial stays, no open houses, no referral incentives; referrals get no special treatment (she's open to recognizing referrers).
- Decline and move-out reasons are asked but **anecdotal, not aggregated, even in a spreadsheet**. Reasons given: cost, room availability, location, family relocating, conflicts with neighbors, death.
- Prospects tend to choose elsewhere for fit (lifestyle, location, care needs), not dislike (S2, ~10:08).
- Short-term/rehab stays are a choice not to offer, because turnover cost is high.

**Operations**
- Supplies and national food contracts (for example Sodexo) are centralized at the central office; produce/protein and **property maintenance are mostly local** (hard to centralize; Cintas supplies can be centralized).
- Activities: a team and lead per community; regional leads coordinate; mostly resident-only; families join big events (holiday, summer picnic).
- Transfers between communities are ad hoc and rare.
- Family communication: no official channel after move-in.

**Technology and data**
- Back-of-house software mainly for medical records (nurses document medications, behavior, interactions); records shared across communities, access limited to those who need it (HIPAA/privacy).
- **No resident-facing technology.**
- A system that is "technically a CRM" but "antiquated and pretty basic," used for intake and follow-up; she couldn't recall its name (S2, ~21:01). Whether it is one shared instance or per community is **not stated**.
- Outreach/marketing is managed per location and does not roll up to the central office; "not super mature."
- Financial data sits in the central office.
