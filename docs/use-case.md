PRODUCT CONCEPT & PROTOTYPE DIRECTION 

Senior Living Operations & Care Intelligence Hub 

A role-aware central hub that helps care and operations teams identify emerging risks, understand the signals behind them, and take action earlier. 

North-star concept 

Design a central intelligence hub for senior living leaders that surfaces meaningful changes across resident care, family engagement, workforce health, and operations. Rather than requiring users to interpret dashboards, the experience should identify what needs attention, explain the signals behind it, and help the appropriate person take action or communicate it to the next shift.

1. Product Idea 

Create a centralized hub for healthcare and operations staff in senior living communities that brings together signals about: 

Resident and family engagement 

Complaints and recurring concerns 

Care delivery and operational efficiency 

Staffing and workforce retention 

Turnover, overtime, scheduling, and rotations 

Shift-to-shift care continuity 

Experience objective 

Help users move from “What’s happening?” to “Why should I care?” to “What needs my attention?” 

 

2. Core Problem 

Important signals currently live across different systems, reports, schedules, complaints, and handoffs. This makes it difficult for leadership and frontline teams to quickly identify: 

Residents or families becoming disengaged 

Patterns in complaints or care concerns 

Workforce retention risks 

Teams or shifts experiencing excessive overtime 

Scheduling or rotation problems 

Changes in care that the next shift needs to know 

Operational trends that may ultimately affect quality of life for residents 

The prototype should explore how these signals can be consolidated into one actionable view rather than another reporting dashboard.

Prototype priority: Design first for an Executive Director / Director of Nursing persona because this role sits at the intersection of care and operations. 

4. Prototype Goal 

Demonstrate that a user can open the hub and answer three questions within roughly 30 seconds: 

What needs my attention? Surface the highest-priority resident, family, workforce, and operational signals. 

What is happening? Provide enough context to understand why something has been flagged. 

What should I do next? Allow the user to investigate the issue or take an appropriate follow-up action. 

This keeps the prototype focused on decision-making rather than trying to visualize every possible metric. 

 

5. Recommended Prototype Structure 

Screen 1: Home / Command Center 

Purpose: Provide an immediate picture of the community. Prioritize exceptions and risks, not a wall of KPIs. 

Needs your attention: resident disengagement, family complaint patterns, elevated overtime, and turnover risk. 

Care: resident engagement, family engagement, complaint patterns, and care-related alerts. 

Workforce: turnover, overtime, schedule coverage, rotation patterns, and retention risk. 

Today’s snapshot: staffing coverage, open shifts, new complaints, and residents requiring follow-up. 

Screen 2: Resident / Family Risk 

Purpose: Help a user understand why a resident or family has been surfaced. 

Example alert: Margaret Thompson - Family engagement declining. 

Signals: no family interaction recorded in 18 days, two recent concerns, a change in participation, and a care-note trend. 

Why this was flagged: a concise, plain-language summary of the combined signals. 

Recent activity: timeline of relevant interactions, complaints, care events, and communications. 

Actions: Review details, Assign follow-up, Contact family. 

Screen 3: Workforce Risk 

Purpose: Identify workforce conditions that may lead to burnout or turnover. 

Example unit: Night Shift - Memory Care. 

Signals: overtime at 14% and increasing, five open shifts, nine schedule changes this week, increasing turnover, and coverage below target. 

What’s driving this: summarize the relationship among overtime, schedule instability, open shifts, and turnover. 

For the prototype, demonstrate understandable combined risk rather than a sophisticated predictive model. 

Screen 4: Shift Handoff 

Purpose: Turn care and operational information into a concise, role-appropriate transition summary. 

Residents needing attention, including what changed and what the next shift should monitor. 

Community updates such as maintenance issues, staffing changes, and agency assignments. 

Action: View full handoff. 

6. Key Design Principle: Role-Based Intelligence 

The same underlying data should produce different, role-relevant experiences: 

Role 

Example insight 

Executive Director 

Family complaints increased 18% this month. 

Director of Nursing 

Four complaints relate to medication communication and evening care. 

Shift nurse 

Two residents on your shift require follow-up related to medication changes. 

Product principle 

The product should not simply say, “Here is the data.” It should say, “Here is what matters to you right now.” 

 

7. Suggested Information Architecture 

Home 

Care: Residents, Family engagement, Complaints 

Workforce: Staffing, Overtime, Turnover, Retention risk 

Shift Handoff 

Insights: Trends, Emerging risks 

The clickable prototype should not build every destination. This structure establishes the broader product architecture while the prototype proves the highest-value workflow. 

 

8. MVP Prototype Flow 

Prototype five screens around a clear decision-making journey: 

1. Command Center 

See care and workforce issues that require attention. 

2. Care Risk Detail 

Select a resident or family disengagement alert. 

3. Resident Detail 

Understand the signals and create a follow-up. 

4. Workforce Risk Detail 

Investigate overtime, scheduling, and turnover risk. 

5. Shift Handoff Summary 

See how care and operational information becomes a concise summary for the next shift. 

Prototype story 

Identify → Understand → Act → Hand off 

 

9. Working Value Proposition 

Option A - Operational 

See what needs attention across care and workforce, all in one place. Bring resident, family, staffing, and operational signals together so teams can identify risks earlier and act quickly. 

Option B - Intelligence-led 

Turn everyday signals into earlier action. Identify emerging care, engagement, staffing, and retention risks before they become larger problems. 

Option C - Human-centered 

Help your teams focus attention where it matters most. A role-aware view of resident, family, workforce, and operational signals that helps senior living teams protect quality of care and quality of life. 

10. Important Prototype Assumption 

Avoid presenting predictive scores such as “Resident disengagement: 82% risk” or “Employee will leave: 74% probability” unless the team has a validated model behind those numbers. 

For the prototype, use explainable status language such as High attention and connect it to observable signals: 

Complaints have increased 

Engagement has decreased 

Overtime has increased 

Schedule instability is rising 

This keeps the experience intelligent, explainable, and credible, especially because the product touches healthcare and workforce decisions. 

11. Prototype Success Criteria 

A user can identify the top issue in under 30 seconds. 

Every surfaced risk includes understandable evidence or contributing signals. 

Care and workforce details lead to a clear next action. 

The handoff summary demonstrates role-appropriate information continuity. 

The concept feels like an intelligence hub, not another reporting dashboard.
